# Limitations

V1 includes profiles, similarity, rankings, combined audits, repeated cohort-stability diagnostics, and a local interface. Deployment remains deferred. Expert/film-based and predictive validation are unperformed.

Coverage is one historical women's league. DOB, contracts, valuation, tracking and 360 are absent for this cohort. Position observations represent usage, not learned tactical roles. Team/style bias and small role groups remain concerns for later analytics.

All 132 matches were mapped and artifact integrity verified. This does not certify complete video, usable minutes or football correctness. Source evidence includes 61 zero-duration positions, 18 reversed position intervals, two invalid card clocks, an observed position overlap and 427 unclassified pass outcomes. The input records remain `UNVALIDATED`; derived appearances are validated individually. 67 appearances remain `NEEDS_REVIEW` with null usable minutes, and 1,118 unused roster entries are excluded.

Minimum-minute thresholds, broad positional mapping and equal category weights are implemented project conventions requiring later sensitivity analysis. Progression thresholds are implemented project conventions requiring later sensitivity analysis. Source-specific fields are preserved, but untyped extensions require explicit canonical contracts before analytics consumes them.

Stage B selects MIT for owned code; provider restrictions remain in force.
This local preparation does not authorize a public release or redistribute data.

Feature completeness is conservative: any unknown contributing opportunity suppresses the full metric/rate. 186 player-team seasons lack full pass completion metrics; 14 have incomplete creation links. There are 56 partial player-team seasons. Open-play/progression proxies and long-pass thresholds are project conventions, not official ability measures.

Profiles describe relative event usage within this league and broad position group, not objective ability or tactical suitability. AM (2 eligible profiles) and CM (9) fail the ten-player minimum. Complete-case categories can have smaller peer populations than individual metrics. Goalkeeper profiles expose distribution activity only; shot stopping is unsupported. No overall player rating is calculated.

Similarity compares event-volume/style proxies, not player quality. The initial model omits completion/progressive-passing/creation metrics because of completeness limits. Goalkeeper similarity covers distribution only. Correlated metrics, team possession and tactical context are unadjusted; sparse-feature scales and model dimensions influence results. The transformed index is not a probability, distances can be asymmetric, and scores cannot be compared across query populations or feature sets. Sensitivity results are diagnostics rather than scouting validation.

Ranking presets are illustrative priorities, not validated scouting preferences. Common completeness reduces the CM example to three reference players and AM to one, so both are suppressed. Candidate minute/team filters retain the full reference benchmark. Activity/possession bias, correlated components and arbitrary project weights remain limitations. Unsupported age, valuation and contract constraints are rejected; a high requirement score says nothing about affordability or availability.

Repeated stability experiments thin players, not matches/events; they do not estimate sampling uncertainty or predictive accuracy. Twenty salted selections are declared reproducible perturbations. Availability must accompany conditional overlap averages: ST similarity failed in 78 of 280 attempts, and ST ranking failed twice in twenty. Candidate removal explains part of ranking turnover. Six football cases describe observed proxies without external archetype validation.
